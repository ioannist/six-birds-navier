\section{Diagnostics}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments in this section are diagnostics, not proofs. They check that the tooling computes
the quantities of the scaffold consistently, and they ask whether the anti-localization
restriction is plausible in tested regimes. Every artifact is produced by a script listed in the
manifest \nolinkurl{paper/figures_manifest.json} (\cref{app:figure-table}). Each script prints its
configuration and writes the plotted arrays to a companion \texttt{.npz} file, so every figure can be
re-plotted from data, and the fast producers are checked for byte-identical output on rerun in a
fixed software environment. Commands are in \cref{app:how-to-run}; the solver is described in
\cref{app:numerics}.

The channel diagnostics and the sweep use the $2\pi$-periodic box, a random band-limited initial field
($k_{\mathrm{init}}=10$), low-wavenumber forcing ($k_{\mathrm{force}}=2$, amplitude $10^{-2}$),
$\alpha=2$, $\mu=0$, time step $5\times10^{-4}$ and final time $0.6$, with the first $40\%$ of each run
discarded as burn-in in the channel diagnostics.

\subsection{Channel count and delocalization}

The finite-channel route to anti-localization (\cref{lem:channel-antiloc}) needs two things: few
channels, and channels that are spread out. For shells $j=1,\dots,4$ on a $32^3$ grid with
$\nu=10^{-3}$ and three seeds, we collect shell snapshots and compute the rank $m_{95}$ needed to
retain $95\%$ of their uncentered squared norm, both within sliding windows of 20 snapshots and
after pooling all snapshots across time and seeds (\cref{fig:channels}a). Every window is
effectively one-dimensional ($m_{95}=1$ in all 15 windows per shell), while the pooled rank is
3 in every shell. A pooled rank above the windowed one is what one expects when a dominant channel rotates
over time: each window sees one direction, and the pooled data span a few.

For the pooled channels we measure, in each shell, the largest local energy fraction of the channel
shapes and the resulting span bound $\hat\eta_j$ of \cref{lem:channel-antiloc}
(\cref{fig:channels}b, solid). It falls from 0.56 at $j=1$ to 0.0024 at $j=4$. The span is not
the data, however. The worst snapshot leaves a fraction 0.19 of its energy outside the
retained span at $j=4$, and charging that residual through \cref{lem:residual} gives a
full-snapshot bound of 0.23 (dashed). At the finest shell the residual, not the span,
controls the bound. A fixed $95\%$ retention therefore does not show asymptotic anti-localization. For this
residual-charged bound to establish it, both $\hat\eta_j\to0$ and a residual fraction tending to zero
would be needed.

\subsection{Anti-localization sweep}

To see whether shells avoid localization along the flow in the tested regimes, we run the
certificate of \cref{def:spt-legal-cert} on 18 short forced runs: resolutions $N\in\{32,48\}$,
viscosities $\nu\in\{0.004,0.002,0.001\}$ and three seeds, recording snapshots every 30 steps.
All 18 runs complete, so coverage is complete. \Cref{fig:sweep} shows the worst values over the sweep.
The largest concentration factor is $\max_j\kappa_j\approx 5.79$ (at $j=4$) and the largest
burst ratio is 1.17, well below the thresholds $\kappa_\star=10$ and $\mathrm{burst}_\star=50$; the largest
rank $m_{95}$ in any run is 2. Shell $j=5$ exists only on the $48^3$ grid and is empty after
de-aliasing, so it is reported as zero; this expresses the empty-shell convention, not a
high-frequency result.

A related sweep in the repository (\nolinkurl{nsbu29_antilocalization_sweep.py}) reports a
channel-normalized factor $\kappa_j/(j+1)$ instead of the raw factor; on the same runs its maximum is
1.16. The two factors answer different questions and should not be compared to the same threshold.

\subsection{Route-capacity and frequency-response diagnostics}

The route-capacity diagnostics (\cref{sec:route-capacity}) extract capacity-like ratios along a
ladder of truncations ($N=8,12,16,24,32$; $k_{\mathrm{init}}=3$, $\nu=10^{-3}$, final time $0.2$) and compare a direct elimination with an explicit
two-stage nested composition. For the instantaneous field-level composition the two agree to
round-off: the measured mismatch is of order $10^{-20}$, as the exact nesting of sharp projections
predicts. The sampled step factors of the capacity ratio lie between 0.60 and 0.71 over
the three available steps; two step factors are unavailable, one because there is no preceding
level and one because the preceding capacity is zero. These are finite-window samples. They do not
establish bounded mismatch for the packaged history map or a polynomial law.

The optional frequency-response diagnostics ($32^3$ run with $k_{\mathrm{init}}=8$, $\nu=10^{-3}$, final
time $0.6$, coarse cutoff at $16^3$) fit a linear time-invariant response to the subgrid term. They use the \emph{extracted}-power convention, opposite in sign to \eqref{eq:power}. In that
convention the real part of the measured response is mostly negative in two of the three tested
shells, so the response is not passive there, and only the remaining shell is eligible for a
passive rational fit. Its root-mean-square
error is about $0.24$; with each frequency's residual weighted by $\sqrt{d_i/\max_i d_i}$, where $d_i$
is the shell input power at that frequency (the denominator of the estimate), it is about $1.8\times10^{-3}$. The fit misses much of the high-frequency data. Nonnegative fitted coefficients certify the
passivity of the chosen rational model, not of the nonlinear Navier--Stokes bridge.
