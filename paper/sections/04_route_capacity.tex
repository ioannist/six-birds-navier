\section{Route stability limits capacity growth}\label{sec:route-capacity}

DIV needs a capacity that grows slowly with depth. One place where the Six Birds viewpoint suggests
leverage beyond one-shot estimates is \emph{route dependence}. A route is an order in which scales
are eliminated. If eliminating two scales at once is close to eliminating them one at a time, the
capacity cannot inflate quickly from one depth to the next. This section turns that idea into an
abstract inequality and proves it in Lean.

\subsection{Capacity recursion with mismatch}

Let $C:\N\to\R$ be a \emph{nonnegative} sequence, read as the effective capacity after $n$
reduction steps, and let $e:\N\to\R$ be a nonnegative mismatch sequence in the same currency.
Fix $p\in\N$ and assume the one-step recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:route-rec}
C(n+1)\ \le\ r(n)\,C(n)+e(n),
\end{equation}
where the step factor is \emph{tame}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-tame}
r(n)\ \le\ \Bigl(\frac{n+2}{n+1}\Bigr)^p.
\end{equation}
The tame factors telescope, $\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl((k+2)/(k+1)\bigr)^p=(n+1)^p$, so multiplicative
inflation along the ladder is at most polynomial. The remaining question is whether the additive
mismatch accumulates. We ask for bounded partial sums:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:e-bounded}
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e(k)\ \le\ E\qquad\text{for all }n,\ \text{for some }E\ge0.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Route stability gives polynomial capacity]\label{thm:route-capacity}
Let $C\ge0$ and $e\ge0$ satisfy \eqref{eq:route-rec} with tame factors \eqref{eq:r-tame}. Then for all $n$
\[
C(n)\ \le\ \Bigl(C(0)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e(k)\Bigr)(n+1)^p.
\]
If moreover \eqref{eq:e-bounded} holds, then $C(n)\le(C(0)+E)(n+1)^p$, so $C$ grows at most
polynomially in the depth.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $C(n)\ge0$, the tame bound may replace $r(n)$ in \eqref{eq:route-rec}:
$C(n+1)\le((n+2)/(n+1))^pC(n)+e(n)$. Iterating, each mismatch term is transported forward by later
tame factors, whose products are at most $(n+1)^p$, and the bound follows by induction.
\end{proof}

Nonnegativity of every $C(n)$ is used in the first step. Without it, an upper bound on $r(n)$ does
not bound $r(n)C(n)$.

With $p\le1$ and a constant work quantum, \cref{thm:route-capacity} produces a capacity that
satisfies the DIV comparison of \cref{sec:no-zeno-engine}. For $p\ge2$ the bound alone does not
decide DIV.

\subsection{Aggregating channels}

A capacity can also be assembled from parts. If the port at depth $j$ splits into $m_j$ atoms,
with powers $a_1,\dots,a_{m_j}$ summing to the total power, then pointwise in time
$\max\bigl(\sum_r a_r,0\bigr)\le\sum_r\max(a_r,0)$. If every atom satisfies ICAP with the same
constant $\Lambda_0$, the aggregate satisfies ICAP with $\Lambda(j)\le m_j\Lambda_0$. Both steps are
mechanized (\nolinkurl{max_sum_le_sum_max}, \nolinkurl{icap_sum_le_card_mul} in
\nolinkurl{formal/Fluids/ECTCap.lean}). For unforced Navier--Stokes, $B(j)=\|u_0\|_{L^2}^2$ then gives
$\mathrm{Cap}(j)\le\|u_0\|_{L^2}^2\Lambda_0 m_j$. With $m_j\le C_0(j+1)$ this is linear in $j$, and
DIV holds. No Navier--Stokes atom decomposition with a uniform per-atom constant is constructed
here; that is an open hypothesis.

\subsection{Interpretation}

In Six Birds language, \eqref{eq:route-rec} says that each additional depth step may inflate the
capacity by a tame factor and pays a route-mismatch penalty for the noncommutativity of
reductions. Condition \eqref{eq:e-bounded} says that this mismatch does not build up, so there is
no hidden ``holonomy'' that grows with depth. \Cref{thm:route-capacity} proves no statement about
the PDE. It shows that \emph{if} a system admits route-stable packaging in this sense, exponential
capacity growth is ruled out by algebra alone.

The repository includes diagnostics that estimate capacity-like sequences along a Navier--Stokes
truncation ladder (\nolinkurl{nsbu21_route_capacity_numbers.py}) and compare their step factors
and mismatch with the hypotheses above (\nolinkurl{nsbu23_route_capacity_hypothesis_check.py}).
They use the supplied-power sign of \eqref{eq:power} and an explicit two-stage nested composition.
Their results are sampled, finite-window ratios. A decaying normalized mismatch, or a step factor
within a fixed tolerance over finitely many depths, establishes neither \eqref{eq:e-bounded} nor a
polynomial law. \Cref{sec:experiments} reports them with that status.
