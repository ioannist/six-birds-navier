\section{The No-Zeno engine}\label{sec:no-zeno-engine}

This section isolates the inequality logic of the scaffold. Nothing in it is specific to
Navier--Stokes. It applies to any multiscale process whose refinement events come with an
increment lower bound and a divergence check. The theorems are mechanized in Lean in a discrete
setting: crossing times form a sequence $t:\N\to\R$ and all hypotheses are stated with finite sums.
The continuous-time arguments that would produce those hypotheses are separate. The one used here
is proved on paper in \cref{lem:work-quantum}.

\subsection{Crossing times and Zeno cascades}

Let $t:\N\to\R$ be a sequence of crossing times, where $t(n)$ is read as the time at which the
frontier reaches level $n$, and write $\Delta t_n:=t(n+1)-t(n)$.

\begin{definition}[Zeno cascade]\label{def:zeno}
A \emph{Zeno cascade} occurs if the crossing times are bounded above: there is $T\in\R$ with
$t(n)\le T$ for every $n$. In words, the frontier reaches every level before a finite time.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Increment certificate]\label{def:phi}
A sequence $\Phi:\N\to\R$ is an \emph{increment certificate} for $t$ if
$\Phi(n)\le t(n+1)-t(n)$ for every $n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Unbounded partial sums exclude a Zeno cascade]\label{thm:unbounded-sums-no-zeno}
Let $\Phi$ be an increment certificate for $t$ whose partial sums are unbounded: for every
$M\in\R$ there is $n$ with $M\le\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Phi(k)$. Then $t$ has no finite upper bound, so
no Zeno cascade occurs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The increment inequality telescopes to $t(n)\ge t(0)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Phi(k)$, and the right-hand
side is unbounded.
\end{proof}

If, in addition, every $\Phi(n)$ is nonnegative, then $t$ is nondecreasing; Lean proves this as a
separate lemma. The theorem itself needs neither monotonicity nor nonnegativity.

\subsection{WORK, CAP and DIV}

In applications the increment certificate comes from a work quantum and a capacity.

\begin{definition}[WORK/CAP/DIV]\label{def:work-cap-div}
Let $w,\mathrm{Cap}:\N\to\R$ with $\mathrm{Cap}(n)>0$ for every $n$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{WORK$\times$CAP:} $w(n)\le \mathrm{Cap}(n)\,\bigl(t(n+1)-t(n)\bigr)$ for every $n$.
Here $w(n)$ is the work needed to advance from level $n$ to level $n+1$, and $\mathrm{Cap}(n)$
bounds the work that can be delivered per unit time at level $n$.
\item \textbf{DIV:} the ratios $w(n)/\mathrm{Cap}(n)$ have unbounded partial sums.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Work and capacity give an increment bound]\label{thm:workcap-increment}
If $\mathrm{Cap}(n)>0$ and $w(n)\le\mathrm{Cap}(n)\,(t(n+1)-t(n))$, then
$w(n)/\mathrm{Cap}(n)\le t(n+1)-t(n)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[WORK, CAP and DIV exclude a Zeno cascade]\label{thm:workcap-no-zeno}
Under WORK$\times$CAP and DIV, the crossing times have no finite upper bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{thm:workcap-increment} makes $w/\mathrm{Cap}$ an increment certificate, and
\cref{thm:unbounded-sums-no-zeno} applies.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Dyadic sampling.}
Sometimes crossings are only checked at each doubling of scale. Lean also proves the sampled
version: if $\mathrm{Cap}(2^j)>0$, $w(2^j)\le\mathrm{Cap}(2^j)\,(t(j+1)-t(j))$ for every $j$, and the
ratios $w(2^j)/\mathrm{Cap}(2^j)$ have unbounded partial sums, then $j\mapsto t(j)$ has no finite
upper bound.

\subsection{Where the hypotheses would come from}

The engine is only as useful as its inputs. Two steps connect it to the bridge of
\cref{sec:six_primitives_mapping}.

\begin{lemma}[A storage frontier forces a work quantum]\label{lem:work-quantum}
Assume the storage $S_j$ is continuous, the power $p_j$ is locally integrable, the passivity
inequality $S_j(t)-S_j(s)\le W_j^+[s,t]$ holds, and the crossing $t_{j+1}$ defined in
\cref{sec:capacity-frontier} is attained. Then $W_j^+[t_j,t_{j+1}]\ge\theta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The running maximum $A(t)=\sup_{\tau\in[t_j,t]}(S_j(\tau)-S_j(t_j))$ is continuous, so
$A(t_{j+1})\ge\theta$. By compactness some $\tau^\ast\in[t_j,t_{j+1}]$ attains it, and
$\theta\le S_j(\tau^\ast)-S_j(t_j)\le W_j^+[t_j,\tau^\ast]\le W_j^+[t_j,t_{j+1}]$, since $p_j^+\ge0$.
\end{proof}

Combining \cref{lem:work-quantum} with a capacity bound $W_j^+[t_j,t_{j+1}]\le\mathrm{Cap}(j)\,\Delta t_j$
gives WORK$\times$CAP with $w(j)=\theta$. The lemma is a continuous-time argument. It is not part of
the Lean development, which only performs the division and summation steps.

\paragraph{Checking DIV.}
DIV is a statement about a series, so only the right comparisons decide it. With a constant work
quantum $\theta>0$:
\begin{itemize}
\item if $\mathrm{Cap}(j)\le K(j+1)^\alpha$ eventually, with $K>0$ and $\alpha\le1$, then
$\sum_j\theta/\mathrm{Cap}(j)=\infty$ and DIV holds;
\item if $\mathrm{Cap}(j)\ge k(j+1)^\alpha$ eventually with $k>0$ and $\alpha>1$, or
$\mathrm{Cap}(j)\ge k\,2^{\beta j}$ with $k,\beta>0$, then the series converges and this capacity cannot
certify DIV.
\end{itemize}
An \emph{upper} bound that grows faster than linearly decides nothing: $\mathrm{Cap}(j)=1$ satisfies
every such bound and has a divergent reciprocal series. Conversely, a convergent comparison series
only leaves No-Zeno unproved. It does not show that the actual crossing increments have a finite
sum, and it does not show that blow-up occurs.
