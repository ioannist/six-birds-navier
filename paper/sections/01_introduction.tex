\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Clay Millennium problem for the three-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes equations asks
whether smooth initial data always produce solutions that stay smooth for all time, or whether a
finite-time singularity can form \citep{fefferman2006clay}. Energy methods alone have not settled
the question. The kinetic energy stays bounded, but a bounded energy is compatible with gradients
that become very large in very small regions. Conditional criteria make this precise: if a peak
quantity, such as the maximum vorticity, is integrable in time, the solution cannot blow up
\citep{bkm_1984,serrin1962interior}. What is missing is a proof that such peak control holds for
every smooth initial datum.

This paper does \emph{not} solve the Clay problem. It builds a \emph{decision scaffold}: a precise
chain of statements, each labelled as proved, measured or open. The chain organizes one
multiscale strategy suggested by the Six Birds framework \citep{tsiokos_2026_18365949}. The idea
is to treat the elimination of fine scales as a \emph{packaging} operation. The eliminated degrees
of freedom become a causal ``bridge'' that acts on the retained scales through an interface, and
an energy ledger constrains what the bridge can do. In this picture a singularity would be a
\emph{Zeno cascade}: an activity front that descends through infinitely many dyadic scales in
finite time.

\paragraph{The strategy in one paragraph.}
Label the dyadic scales $j=0,1,2,\dots$ and let $t_j$ be the time at which a suitably defined
frontier first reaches depth $j$. Suppose each advance costs at least a fixed amount of work
$\theta>0$ (WORK). Suppose also that the bridge can deliver work to depth $j$ at a rate of at most
$\mathrm{Cap}(j)$ (CAP). Then the step from depth $j$ to depth $j+1$ takes at least
$\theta/\mathrm{Cap}(j)$ units of time. If these lower bounds have divergent sum (DIV), the crossing
times cannot accumulate at a finite time. The logic is elementary and is mechanized here. The
difficulty lies in producing WORK, CAP and DIV for the actual Navier--Stokes bridge, and in
showing that a blow-up would really push this frontier to infinite depth.

\paragraph{What the analysis shows.}
Two findings shape the rest of the paper. First, a capacity certificate that holds uniformly over
all admissible inputs is an extremal bound. In a finite model, if the admissible inputs include
localized test vectors, such a certificate is exactly as strong as a pointwise bound on the gain
(\cref{thm:icap-no-go}). That is the shape of a classical peak criterion. We do not prove the
continuum analogue for the actual bridge (\cref{sec:no-go-icap} lists what it would need), but the
finite theorem marks the risk: unless the admissible inputs are restricted, a uniform capacity
certificate may be no weaker than peak control.
Second, the information that is always available is too weak to close the chain. Energy alone gives
a capacity that grows like $2^{5j/2}$ in the depth $j$, and the reciprocals of such a capacity have
a finite sum. The natural sharpening, a bound by $\|\nabla u\|_\infty$ times the shell energy, fails for the full subgrid
port: an exact three-mode field gives supplied power proportional to $\varepsilon$ with shell energy
proportional to $\varepsilon^2$ (\cref{prop:sgs-counterexample}).

\paragraph{A restricted problem.}
These findings suggest one escape route inside the scaffold, which we adopt as a proposal: require
admissible inputs to be spread out in space at each scale, so that localized tests are not available. We call such data
\emph{SBT-legal} (``SBT'' for Six Birds Theory). We state the corresponding
\emph{anti-localization} hypothesis and prove, in Lean, that it follows from a finite set of spread-out channels. We also turn
it into a reproducible certificate for initial data. Smooth random band-limited fields pass the
certificate. A band-limited wavepacket concentrated in one small ball exceeds the threshold forty-fold,
about two hundred times the baseline's value. Whether the Navier--Stokes flow preserves legality is open.

\paragraph{Three layers of claims.}
Every statement in the paper belongs to one of three layers.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Mechanized (Lean).} The inequality logic of the No-Zeno engine, the route-capacity
bound, the capacity aggregation step, the finite anti-localization lemma and the finite no-go
theorem. These are system-agnostic statements about sequences and finite sums. The 31 audited
declarations depend only on the standard axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and
\texttt{Quot.sound} (\cref{app:theorem-index}).
\item \textbf{Analytic (paper proofs).} Navier--Stokes-facing statements with complete proofs in
the text: the exact return of the bridge, the energy bound on the subgrid power and its
counterexample, the residual-charged anti-localization bound, and the properties of the
regularized model used in the simulations (\cref{app:regularized}).
\item \textbf{Open or measured.} The hinge lemmas that a regularity proof along these lines would
need are stated as open hypotheses. Numerical diagnostics are reproducible from a manifest. They
are evidence, not proofs.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A mechanized No-Zeno chain and a mechanized route-capacity theorem, with the Lean adapters
that connect each theorem to the form in which it is used (\cref{sec:no-zeno-engine,sec:route-capacity}).
\item A precise packaged-bridge definition for Navier--Stokes, with the sign of the supplied power
fixed and an exact return on strong solutions (\cref{sec:six_primitives_mapping}).
\item The energy-only capacity bound and an explicit counterexample to a shell-only strain bound for the full
subgrid port (\cref{sec:energy-capacity}).
\item A finite no-go theorem that shows why uniform capacity certificates and localization-rich
inputs do not mix, with the continuum passage left explicitly open (\cref{sec:no-go-icap}).
\item An anti-localization hypothesis, its finite-channel proof, and an operational certificate
with a manifest-driven, deterministic figure pipeline (\cref{sec:spt-legal,sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reading guide.}
\Cref{sec:six_primitives_mapping} recalls the Six Birds vocabulary and fixes the Navier--Stokes
objects. \Cref{sec:no-zeno-engine,sec:route-capacity} give the mechanized engine and the route-capacity
theorem. \Cref{sec:energy-capacity} shows what energy alone provides. \Cref{sec:no-go-icap} states
the no-go theorem. \Cref{sec:spt-legal} develops anti-localization and the certificate, and
\cref{sec:experiments} reports the diagnostics. \Cref{sec:discussion} lists what remains open.
The appendices contain the Lean index, the regularized model, the numerical method, the
reproduction commands and a version note.
