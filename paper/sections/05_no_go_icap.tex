\section{Uniform capacity and localized tests do not mix}
\label{sec:no-go-icap}

ICAP \eqref{eq:icap} is an extremal statement: it bounds the positive work over \emph{every}
admissible input. This section shows, in a finite model, what that costs. If the admissible inputs
include localized test vectors, a uniform capacity certificate is exactly as strong as a pointwise
bound on the gain. We then state what a continuum version for the actual bridge would require.

\subsection{A finite obstruction}

Let $\iota$ be a finite index set, such as the cells of a grid, and let $g:\iota\to\R$ be a gain
profile with quadratic form $Q_g(u)=\sum_{i\in\iota}g(i)\,u(i)^2$. Let $\mathcal F$ be a class of
feasible inputs $u:\iota\to\R$. A memoryless ICAP on $\mathcal F$ reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:icap-discrete}
\max\bigl(Q_g(u),0\bigr)\ \le\ \Lambda\sum_{i\in\iota}u(i)^2\qquad\text{for all }u\in\mathcal F .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[ICAP on localized tests gives a pointwise gain bound]\label{thm:icap-no-go}
Assume \eqref{eq:icap-discrete} for some $\Lambda\in\R$, and assume $\mathcal F$ contains every
coordinate vector $\mathbf 1_{\{i\}}$. Then $\max(g(i),0)\le\Lambda$ for every $i\in\iota$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Test \eqref{eq:icap-discrete} with $u=\mathbf 1_{\{i\}}$, for which $Q_g(u)=g(i)$ and $\sum u^2=1$.
\end{proof}

The converse holds as well: if $\max(g(i),0)\le\Lambda$ for every $i$ and $\Lambda\ge0$, then
\eqref{eq:icap-discrete} holds for all $u$. On a localization-rich class, a uniform capacity
certificate and a pointwise gain bound are therefore the same statement.

\subsection{What a continuum version would need}

In the continuum the role of the basis vectors would be played by divergence-free wavepackets in
shell $j$ whose energy concentrates in a ball of radius about $2^{-j}$. If the bridge acted on
such packets through a local gain, a uniform ICAP would bound that gain pointwise in space. For
Navier--Stokes the natural candidate is a strain-type gain, and a pointwise bound on it has the
form of a classical peak criterion \citep{majda_bertozzi_vif,bkm_1984,serrin1962interior}.

We do \emph{not} prove this passage. The actual bridge is nonlinear and history-dependent, and the
finite theorem cannot simply be applied to it. A continuum version would need three adapters,
each open here:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an actual gain representation of the bridge's positive work on shell-$j$ inputs;
\item feasibility of solenoidal localized test inputs in the bridge's admissible class;
\item a limit argument with control of the fixed background, turning localized averages at finite
scale into a pointwise bound on the claimed strain readout.
\end{enumerate}
\Cref{prop:sgs-counterexample} is a warning about item (3). For the full subgrid port, the shell
power is not controlled by $\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}$ times the shell energy at all, because the
high--high source can feed an almost empty shell.

\subsection{Consequence: localization is the lever}

\Cref{thm:icap-no-go} identifies what a capacity route must avoid. If the admissible inputs at
depth $j$ can concentrate in one cell, a uniform certificate inherits the full strength of a
pointwise bound. One way to keep a capacity certificate from inheriting that strength is to
restrict the admissible inputs so that they cannot concentrate. This restriction is a proposal,
not a consequence of the theorem: other restrictions of the input class could also block the
basis tests. The next section makes the anti-localization restriction quantitative. Excluding localized tests only removes the obstruction. It
does not by itself provide ICAP, WORK, DIV, or a continuation criterion.
