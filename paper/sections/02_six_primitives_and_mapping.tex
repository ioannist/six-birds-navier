\section{Six Birds vocabulary and the Navier--Stokes objects}
\label{sec:six_primitives_mapping}

This section introduces the parts of the Six Birds framework \citep{tsiokos_2026_18365949} that the
scaffold uses, and it fixes the Navier--Stokes objects that later sections refer to. For standard
Navier--Stokes background see \citet{temam_ns} or \citet{majda_bertozzi_vif}.

\subsection{Six moves for multiscale elimination}
\label{sec:six_primitives_recap}

The Six Birds framework describes six recurring structural moves. Here they serve as a vocabulary
for eliminating fine scales without committing to a closure model.

\begin{description}
\item[P1, rewrite.] Choose coordinates in which scale is explicit. We use dyadic Fourier shells.
This is a change of description, not a modelling assumption.
\item[P2, feasibility.] Decide which interface signals are admissible. Feasibility appears twice:
in the domain of the packaged bridge, and in the anti-localization restriction of
\cref{sec:spt-legal}.
\item[P3, route dependence.] Eliminating scales in different orders can give different effective
descriptions. \Cref{sec:route-capacity} turns a bound on this mismatch into a bound on capacity
growth.
\item[P4, sectorization.] Interactions may decompose into a small number of channels. We treat the
channel count as a measurable quantity and prove that few spread-out channels force
anti-localization.
\item[P5, packaging.] Turn everything above a cutoff into one causal object, the bridge, that acts
on the retained scales. The bridge is defined below; it is not fitted.
\item[P6, accounting.] Constrain the bridge by an energy ledger, in the spirit of dissipativity
theory \citep{willems1972dissipative1}. The currency is the positive work that the bridge supplies
to a shell.
\end{description}

\subsection{Shells, projections and the closure force}
\label{sec:ns_object_mapping}

Work on the periodic box $\T^3=(\R/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^3$ with zero-mean, divergence-free velocity
fields, viscosity $\nu>0$ and no external force. For $j\ge 0$ let
\[
\mathcal K_j=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^3:\ 2^j\le |k|<2^{j+1}\}.
\]
Let $\Delta_j$ be the \emph{sharp} orthogonal Fourier projection onto the modes in $\mathcal K_j$, let $G_j$
project onto $|k|<2^{j+1}$, and set $H_j=I-G_j$. Every nonzero frequency on this box has $|k|\ge 1$,
so a zero-mean field decomposes as $u=\sum_{j\ge0}\Delta_j u$. We write
\[
u_j:=\Delta_j u,\qquad u_{\le j}:=G_j u,\qquad u_{>j}:=H_j u.
\]
Because the projections are sharp, $\Delta_j^2=\Delta_j$, $G_j^2=G_j$, $\Delta_jG_j=\Delta_j$ and
$G_jH_j=0$, and all of them commute with derivatives and with the Leray projection $\mathcal P$.
Smooth, overlapping Littlewood--Paley multipliers \citep{bahouri2011fourier} do not have these
identities. A smooth-filter version of the constructions below would need a separate argument.

Write $\mathrm{NL}(u):=\mathcal P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)$, so that a strong solution satisfies
$\partial_t u=\nu\Delta u-\mathrm{NL}(u)$. The \emph{closure force} at cutoff $j$ and its shell
readout are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closure}
C_{\le j}(u):=G_j\,\mathrm{NL}(G_ju)-G_j\,\mathrm{NL}(u),\qquad y_j(u):=\Delta_j C_{\le j}(u).
\end{equation}
With these signs the exact retained and shell equations read
\[
\partial_t u_{\le j}=\nu\Delta u_{\le j}-G_j\mathrm{NL}(u_{\le j})+C_{\le j}(u),
\qquad
\partial_t u_j=\nu\Delta u_j-\Delta_j\mathrm{NL}(u_{\le j})+y_j(u).
\]
Thus $y_j$ is the force that the eliminated scales add to shell $j$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power}
p_j(t):=\langle u_j(t),y_j(t)\rangle_{L^2},\qquad p_j^+:=\max(p_j,0),\qquad
W_j^+[s,t]:=\int_s^t p_j^+(\tau)\,d\tau
\end{equation}
are the power \emph{supplied} to the shell, its positive part, and the positive supplied work.
The sign matters. Reversing it exchanges supplied and extracted power and changes $W_j^+$.

\subsection{The packaged bridge}

The bridge packages the eliminated scales as a causal map on resolved histories.

\begin{definition}[Packaged bridge $Z_j$]\label{def:bridge}
Fix $s>5/2$, $T>0$ and $u_0\in H^s$. A resolved history $v\in C([0,T];\mathrm{Ran}\,G_j)$ is
\emph{admissible} if the high-mode problem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:high-ivp}
\partial_t w=\nu\Delta w-H_j\,\mathrm{NL}(v+w),\qquad w(0)=H_ju_0,
\end{equation}
has a unique strong solution $w\in C([0,T];H^s)\cap L^2(0,T;H^{s+1})$. For admissible $v$ set
\[
Z_j[v](t):=\Delta_j\bigl(\mathrm{NL}(v(t))-\mathrm{NL}(v(t)+w(t))\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

The input of $Z_j$ is the whole resolved history $v$, not only its shell-$j$ part. The power
pairs the output with $\Delta_j v$. Changing other resolved shells can change the output while
leaving $\Delta_j v$ untouched, so the bridge does not in general factor through a single-port map.
We do not claim that every candidate history is admissible.

\begin{proposition}[Causality and exact return]\label{prop:bridge-return}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item The high-mode range is invariant: a solution of \eqref{eq:high-ivp} satisfies $H_jw=w$.
\item If two admissible histories agree on $[0,t]$, the corresponding high-mode solutions and
bridge outputs agree on $[0,t]$.
\item If $u$ is a strong Navier--Stokes solution on $[0,T]$, then $v=G_ju$ is admissible and
$Z_j[G_ju](t)=y_j(u(t))$ for $t\in[0,T]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) Applying $G_j$ to \eqref{eq:high-ivp} leaves a heat equation for $G_jw$ with zero data, so
$G_jw=0$. (2) Strong solutions on $[0,t]$ are unique. For two solutions $w_1,w_2$ driven by the
same history, the difference $z$ obeys an $L^2$ energy estimate. The transport term with the full
velocity cancels, $H_j$ drops out of the pairing with $z$, and the remaining terms are bounded by
the strong gradients of $v,w_1,w_2$; Gr\"onwall gives $z=0$. (3) Applying $H_j$ to the
Navier--Stokes equation shows that $w=H_ju$ solves \eqref{eq:high-ivp} with $v=G_ju$, and
uniqueness identifies it with the packaged high state. Since $\Delta_j=\Delta_jG_j$, the definition
of $Z_j$ then coincides with $y_j(u)$ in \eqref{eq:closure}.
\end{proof}

Frequency-response fits of the subgrid term are finite-window, linear time-invariant
approximations. They neither define $Z_j$ nor certify any property of it over its admissible class.

\subsection{Capacity and frontier}\label{sec:capacity-frontier}

Two further objects connect the bridge to the No-Zeno engine of \cref{sec:no-zeno-engine}.

\paragraph{Capacity.} The integrated capacity inequality (ICAP) at depth $j$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:icap}
W_j^+[s,t]\ \le\ \Lambda(j)\int_s^t\|u_j(\tau)\|_{L^2}^2\,d\tau\qquad\text{for all admissible inputs and windows }[s,t].
\end{equation}
It is a certificate quantified over the admissible class, not a bound along one trajectory. If in
addition $\|u_j(t)\|_{L^2}^2\le B(j)$, then $W_j^+[s,t]\le \mathrm{Cap}(j)\,(t-s)$ with
$\mathrm{Cap}(j)=\Lambda(j)B(j)$. For unforced Navier--Stokes the energy inequality gives
$B(j)=\|u_0\|_{L^2}^2$ at every depth.

\paragraph{Frontier.} Let $S_j\ge0$ be a storage functional for the eliminated scales, continuous in
time, and assume the passivity inequality $S_j(t)-S_j(s)\le\int_s^tp_j\le W_j^+[s,t]$. Given a
threshold $\theta>0$, the frontier crosses from depth $j$ to depth $j+1$ at
\[
t_{j+1}:=\inf\Bigl\{t\ge t_j:\ \sup_{\tau\in[t_j,t]}\bigl(S_j(\tau)-S_j(t_j)\bigr)\ge\theta\Bigr\}.
\]
Writing an infimum does not make the crossing exist. We call a crossing \emph{attained} when the
set is nonempty and $t_{j+1}$ is finite and lies in the time domain. A frontier index
$j_\ast(t)=\sup\{j:t_j\le t\}$ may be infinite; assuming that it is a finite maximum would assume
away the Zeno accumulation that the scaffold is meant to rule out.

The storage functional, the passivity inequality and the capacity bound are hypotheses about the
actual bridge. None of them is proved for Navier--Stokes here.
